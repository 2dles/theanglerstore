*Researched August 2026. Everything quoted was read off a live page.*

## ⚠️ Start here: 2–3 day delivery, honestly

**You cannot promise 2–3 day nationwide delivery on a dropship model.** Not without paying for air freight on every order. Here's why, and it's a geography problem more than a supplier problem:

| Supplier | Warehouses | 2–3 days to… | 2–3 days to California? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CWR** | Bayville NJ + Tampa FL | Northeast, Southeast, Mid-Atlantic | ❌ Not by ground. $49.95 flat overnight only. |
| **Burch** | Florence AL (single) | Southeast, lower Midwest | ❌ 4–5 days ground |
| **Hicks** | Luverne AL (single) | Southeast | ❌ 4–5 days |
| **Printful** | Charlotte NC + Coppell TX | Southeast, Texas, lower Midwest | ⚠️ 5–9 days total (production + transit) |
| **Land 'N' Sea** | **15 warehouses incl. Fresno CA, Seattle WA** | ✅ Nearly everywhere | ✅ Yes — but they sell boat parts, not tackle |

### The problem nobody has named yet

**Every viable tackle distributor is East Coast. Your traffic is California.**

USTideCharts is a California pilot — 20 California locations. The people clicking your gear cards are in Monterey, Half Moon Bay, Santa Cruz. Every distributor that will take a small online-only account ships from New Jersey, Florida, or Alabama.

That is a **4–6 day ground transit** to your actual customer base, on top of dispatch time.

### What you can honestly say

| Promise | Achievable? |
|---|---|
| "2–3 business days nationwide" | ❌ No. Don't. |
| "2–5 business days East of the Mississippi, 5–8 West" | ✅ Yes, with CWR |
| "Most orders arrive in 3–7 business days" | ✅ Yes, defensible |
| "Ships within 1 business day" | ✅ Yes — CWR ships same-day before 4pm EST |

**Lead with dispatch speed, not delivery speed.** "Ships same day" is true, verifiable, and it's what you actually control.

### Three ways to actually get fast West Coast delivery

1. **Amazon Multi-Channel Fulfillment (MCF).** Buy inventory, send it to Amazon, they fulfill *your website's* orders from the FC nearest the customer. This is the only realistic path to genuine 2–3 day nationwide. Costs: you buy inventory upfront, plus per-unit fulfillment fees. Worth investigating once you know what sells.
2. **A West Coast 3PL.** Same principle, more control, more admin. Only sensible above meaningful volume.
3. **Stock a small amount yourself.** Nicklow's ($50 minimum, resale certificate, residential address accepted) lets you buy real wholesale terminal tackle and ship it from your own house. You'd be 1–2 days from every California customer. This is the cheapest way to get genuinely fast on your best-selling small items.

**Recommendation:** don't chase 2–3 days at launch. Ship honestly, find out what sells, then put *those* SKUs somewhere fast.

## Two things that will surprise you about wholesale

### 1. Your margins will be worse than the plan assumes

Keystone (50% off MSRP) is what **high-volume** dealers get. A brand-new account with no purchase history typically opens at **30–40% off MSRP**. Distributors take a cut of that, so buying Shimano through CWR costs more than buying it factory-direct. Then per-order freight ($9.95–$49.95) comes straight off a small order.

**Small dropshipped orders on branded tackle can net under 10%.**

No distributor publishes a dealer discount schedule. **You cannot know your real cost until you're approved.** Treat the margins in `SOURCING.md` as provisional until you see an actual price file.

### 2. A wholesale account is not permission to sell the good brands

MAP is typically set 10–20% below MSRP. Combined with a 30–40% opening discount, your advertised margin is capped around 24–35% and you can't discount your way to sales.

Worse, marquee brands require **separate internet-reseller agreements**. Rather Outdoors (Lew's, Strike King, Zebco, Quantum) requires Internet Reseller Agreements plus Unilateral Pricing Policies. Expect **Shimano, Daiwa, St. Croix, G.Loomis-tier brands to be unavailable** to a new online-only account.

**Your realistic opening assortment is second-tier and value brands, terminal tackle, line, soft plastics, accessories — and apparel.** Which are also the categories with the better margins. That's not a consolation prize; it's the actual business.

⚠️ **Middleware warning:** Inventory Source shut down in August 2025 and migrated users to Flxpoint at **$599–$2,000/month**. Several distributors' feeds route through that. **Get a direct free FTP/CSV feed from CWR and skip middleware entirely.**

## The plan

### This week — costs nothing, needs no approval

1. **Open a free Printful account.** Stores → **Connect via API**.
2. **Get a logo file ready** and pay the **$6.50 digitization fee once**. It's per design, not per product.
3. **Order samples**: one Yupoong 6606 trucker, one Bella+Canvas tee, one neck gaiter. ~$40. Time the delivery yourself before promising anything.
4. **Photograph the samples when they arrive.** Mockups get you launched; a real hat on a real jetty is what sells, and unlike mockups you own those photos forever and can take them to any supplier.

### Same week — free, but slow to return

5. **Apply to CWR** (https://cwrdistribution.com/signup). Have ready: business license, EIN, state resale certificate, personal guarantee, and your URL as "sales venue." Take the credit-card account.
6. **Apply to Burch and Hicks** with the five questions above.

### On CWR approval

7. **Pull the image library immediately** — Tools → Download Images / Export Data — and request free FTP integration.
8. **Get the image permission in writing from your rep.** Their terms don't have an explicit licence clause; a one-line "yes, that's what they're for" email is the artifact you'd want if anyone ever asked.
9. **Rebuild the catalog against the real price file.** The 14 keys stay — they're the URL contract with USTideCharts — but names, prices, and margins get redone from actual cost.

### Then, once you know what sells

10. **Move the winners somewhere fast.** Amazon MCF or a small self-stocked inventory from Nicklow's. Fix delivery speed for the SKUs that earn it, not for the whole catalog.
